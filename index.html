Kenya Climate & Nature Directory · Sole engineer · 2026

A studio that turns research into explainer videos and posts

The problem
The Kenya Climate & Nature Directory holds thousands of research papers, but research only helps people once it reaches them in a form they can take in.
The goal
Let one editor turn a paper into ready-to-share content (posts, images and a short narrated video) without a designer or a video editor.
My role
Sole engineer: I built the directory's research pipeline and its studios, from the database to the editor's dashboard.
How I built it
The editor searches the whole library and picks a paper. AI drafts posts from it in the tone the editor chooses. The studio then renders carousel images, a short explainer video with a synthetic voice-over, and a download bundle. Rendering runs as a queued background job, and the files are stored in the cloud. If one AI provider fails, it switches to another, and a test checks that the models it depends on are still available.
The challenges
A free AI provider closed and a model was retired without warning, which took the studio down. I added automatic fallback between providers and a live health check, so the next change shows up before an editor hits it.
The outcome
It now also runs the Climate Shapers Africa studio, on a fixed weekly rhythm: a news post with a 60–90 second narrated video every morning, research carousels three times a week, and a weekly bulletin and newsletter, all on one editorial calendar where nothing is posted automatically. It is part of the directory's production system, alongside a nightly research pipeline that searches six sources (2,774 records migrated), a News Studio and a grants tracker. Every item is approved by a person before it is published.
What I learned
Video and plain language are what carry information to people. The technology's job is to make that fast for the person creating it and safe for the person receiving it.

British International Investment · Prototype · 2026

Turning a long technical framework into a guided, step-by-step tool

The problem
A digital version of British International Investment’s climate and nature risk toolkit for financial institutions.
The goal
Turn guidance that lived in PDFs and spreadsheets into tools that banks and investors can use in the middle of a real lending or investment decision.
My role
Product lead and sole engineer: turned the framework into a product plan, then designed and built the prototype.
How we shipped it
I broke the toolkit into modules and built step-by-step workbenches for each task: screening a client for climate risk, measuring the emissions linked to loans and investments, and stress-testing a portfolio. Each piece of work links institutions, clients and projects, and exports cleanly.
The challenges
The source guidance is long and technical, and the people using the tool are finance staff, not climate specialists. It had to stay faithful to the method while feeling simple.
The outcome
A working prototype covering all 23 modules of the toolkit, with guided journeys, calculators, linked records and exports. It is now in proposal and review.
What I learned
Complex frameworks get used when they are broken into small guided steps. Most of the work is understanding the method well enough to make it simple.

Federal Ministry of Education, Nigeria · ProDG Studios · 2026

A national platform to move research into the market

The problem
RICC, a national platform for Nigeria’s Research, Innovation and Commercialisation Council.
The goal
Connect universities, industry, innovators and regulators so more research turns into real products and services.
My role
Frontend and UX lead at ProDG Studios: stakeholder research, user journeys for every group, QA and rollout readiness.
How we shipped it
I led the frontend and user experience. Before designing anything, I researched where collaboration was breaking down, then built the user journeys around those bottlenecks. The portal works offline: field agents fill in questionnaires where there is no internet, and their answers are sent once they are back online. I built the landing page, the analytics dashboards that show where innovations stall on the way to market, the evidence-verification panel and the consent controls, and I ran end-to-end testing before the national rollout.
The challenges
Many groups with different needs had to be served by one platform: universities, industry, innovators, regulators and funders. Much of the data had to be collected in places with weak or no internet, and the same innovation was often reported more than once.
The outcome
A live national platform for the Ministry of Education and TETFund, with questionnaires for every type of stakeholder, ready for a national rollout.
What I learned
Understand the problem before you build. Putting a broken paper process online only gives you a faster broken process.

Nairobi City County · Pneuma Labs · 2026

A WhatsApp assistant for a county revenue team

The problem
An assistant that county revenue staff can message on WhatsApp to get answers about the revenue the county has collected.
The goal
Let staff get a revenue figure in seconds by asking in plain words, instead of digging through spreadsheets.
My role
Product and engineering lead: scoped it with the revenue team, built it and checked every answer against the source.
How we shipped it
I built a system that reads the county’s revenue reports into one clean database, then added an AI assistant staff can message on WhatsApp or the web. The AI can only read the data, never change it, and only approved phone numbers can use it.
The challenges
The reports came in several different layouts, and the same money sometimes appeared in more than one report. The AI also had to be useful without ever being able to change the county’s records.
The outcome
Staff ask questions like “what did this revenue stream bring in last quarter?” and get answers from the county’s own records. I checked the answers against the original reports and fixed the places where amounts were being counted twice.
What I learned
The hard part was not the AI, it was the data. An answer that looks right is not the same as an answer that is right, so every number has to be checked against the source.

PropFlow · Pneuma Labs · 2026

AI and automation that run a landlord’s routine work

The problem
An AI and automated property-management system for landlords and managing agents.
The goal
Take the routine work of property management off landlords’ hands, so they get reports instead of chasing tasks.
My role
Product lead and lead engineer, with a partner: product vision, roadmap, partner onboarding in Uganda and Rwanda, and the build.
How we shipped it
I designed and built it with a partner. The rent roll, invoicing and billing, arrears and late fees, deposits, reconciliation and tenant receipts run automatically, leases are generated and signed online, and maintenance requests go to contractors with approvals. Landlords can ask for reports on WhatsApp, tenants report problems there, and an emergency triggers a phone call. AI proposes the next step, but code checks each suggestion against strict rules before anything happens. I also travelled to Uganda and Rwanda to meet partners, including the leadership teams of Keystone Technologies and Unispace.
The challenges
The system handles real money and legal documents, and tenant messages are unpredictable. Every automated step and every AI suggestion had to be safe before it could reach a landlord or tenant.
The outcome
Live with users in Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda. Built for production: 90 data models, 120 test files, online payments, nightly backups and a written recovery plan. In Rwanda I trained five CEOs, property owners and their CTOs to use it, and I maintain it in production.
What I learned
Automation earns trust when AI suggests and clear rules decide. That is what makes it safe to put near tenants and landlords’ money.

Market intelligence · Pneuma Labs · 2026

An AI research assistant for Nairobi Securities Exchange analysts

The problem
An AI tool that does the first round of stock research on the Nairobi Securities Exchange.
The goal
Cut the time analysts spend gathering prices, economic data and news before they can start thinking about a stock.
My role
Product and engineering lead: defined what analysts needed, built the agents and set up accuracy tracking.
How we shipped it
I adapted an open-source framework into a team of AI “analysts” that each study one angle, argue the case for and against a stock, then pass a risk check that accounts for Kenyan inflation. It runs automatically every morning before the market opens, and switches to a backup AI provider if one goes down.
The challenges
The data comes from many scattered sources, AI providers sometimes go down, and nobody trusts an AI’s stock call unless its track record can be checked.
The outcome
Analysts used it for research and decision support, and it was later sold to another business, where it still runs. It does the groundwork; the investment decision stays with them.
What I learned
You can’t trust an AI you can’t measure. Logging every prediction and scoring it later is what turns a clever demo into a tool people rely on.

Fintech · Diaspora investment · 2025 – 2026

An investment marketplace that partner apps can plug in

The problem
A cross-border investment marketplace for people in the diaspora, built for a fintech client.
The goal
Let people abroad invest in vetted African opportunities from inside apps they already use, such as money-transfer and banking apps, with trust and compliance built in.
My role
Senior Software Engineer at ProDG Studios, with product work throughout: the roadmap audit, the workflow explainer, the API specification, QA and user acceptance testing.
How we shipped it
I developed and deployed the frontend, integrated the platform with IMTOs (international money transfer operators) so their customers could invest from apps they already use, took it through user acceptance testing into production, and kept working on the product into 2026, across research, product design and QA. The product covers investor onboarding with identity checks, the marketplace, wallet funding, a portal for fund managers to list investments, partner dashboards and an admin console.
The challenges
A key provider changed partway through, so money movement had to work with any provider and be tested against a stand-in. Business verification was blocked, so we used a manual route. And with investors’ money involved, every amount had to reconcile exactly.
The outcome
Signed off in user acceptance testing and deployed to production, with identity checks live and money-transfer partners integrated.
What I learned
In fintech, trust is the product. Getting every amount to add up matters more than any feature, and building so the payment provider can change keeps a project alive when partners change.

How I work

How I deliver a project, from scope to support.

The same five steps on every engagement, whether it's a national portal or a tool for one editor. Each comes with an example from real work.

Understand

Talk to the people who will use it and see how the work really happens, before deciding what to build.

On RICC I researched why universities, industry, investors and regulators weren't connecting, then wrote user journeys for each group.

Scope

Agree a clear piece of work: what it does, what it doesn't, and how we'll know it's done. Engineers can build from it without guessing.

On CrossVest I wrote the workflow explainer and the frontend-to-backend API specification.

Build

Working software every week you can click through, with honest status. When a date matters, I cut scope on purpose rather than let quality slip.

When CrossVest's payment provider was removed mid-build, we made payments provider-neutral and cut scope to hit the demo date.

Test

Nothing ships until it has been tested against real cases and signed off by the people who will use it.

A 142-case test pack across 20 modules for RICC; 120 test files on PropFlow; CrossVest signed off in user acceptance testing.

Ship and support

Launch with the people who use it, train them, and keep it running: fixes, monitoring and the next round of improvements.

I trained five CEOs and their CTOs in Rwanda on PropFlow and have maintained it since launch; NGAO grew past 3,000 users after rollout.

I build for the people at the end of the line: the field worker with no signal, the editor with no designer, the staff member who just needs an answer.
