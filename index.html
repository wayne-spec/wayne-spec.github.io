Nairobi City County · Pneuma Labs · 2026

A WhatsApp assistant for a county revenue team

The project
An assistant that county revenue staff can message on WhatsApp to get answers about the revenue the county has collected.
The goal
Let staff get a revenue figure in seconds by asking in plain words, instead of digging through spreadsheets.
How we did it
I built a system that reads the county’s revenue reports into one clean database, then added an AI assistant staff can message on WhatsApp or the web. The AI can only read the data, never change it, and only approved phone numbers can use it.
The challenges
The reports came in several different layouts, and the same money sometimes appeared in more than one report. The AI also had to be useful without ever being able to change the county’s records.
The outcome
Staff ask questions like “what did this revenue stream bring in last quarter?” and get answers from the county’s own records. I checked the answers against the original reports and fixed the places where amounts were being counted twice.
What I learned
The hard part was not the AI, it was the data. An answer that looks right is not the same as an answer that is right, so every number has to be checked against the source.

Fintech · Confidential client · 2025 – 2026

An investment marketplace that partner apps can plug in

The project
A cross-border investment marketplace for people in the diaspora, built for a confidential fintech client.
The goal
Let people abroad invest in vetted African opportunities from inside apps they already use, such as money-transfer and banking apps, with trust and compliance built in.
How we did it
I developed and deployed the frontend, took it through user acceptance testing into production, and kept working on the product into 2026, across research, product design and QA. The product covers investor onboarding with identity checks, the marketplace, wallet funding, a portal for fund managers to list investments, partner dashboards and an admin console.
The challenges
A key provider changed partway through, so money movement had to work with any provider and be tested against a stand-in. Business verification was blocked, so we used a manual route. And with investors’ money involved, every amount had to reconcile exactly.
The outcome
The frontend was delivered, signed off in user acceptance testing and deployed to production, with identity checks live.
What I learned
In fintech, trust is the product. Getting every amount to add up matters more than any feature, and building so the payment provider can change keeps a project alive when partners change.

PropFlow · Pneuma Labs · 2026

One system for landlords to run rent, tenants and repairs

The project
Property-management software for landlords and managing agents in East Africa.
The goal
Replace the spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads and paper files landlords juggle with one system for rent, tenants and repairs.
How we did it
I designed and built the whole platform: rent and billing, arrears, deposits, leases signed online, repairs with contractor approvals and online payments. Tenants report problems on WhatsApp, and an emergency triggers a phone call. AI reads tenant messages and suggests what to do, but the system checks each suggestion against the rules before acting. I also travelled to Uganda and Rwanda to meet partners, including the leadership teams of Keystone Technologies and Unispace.
The challenges
Tenant messages are unpredictable, and the system handles real money and legal documents. Every AI suggestion had to be safe before it could reach a landlord or tenant.
The outcome
A production-ready platform with online payments, automated testing, nightly backups and a written recovery plan. In Rwanda I trained five CEOs, property owners and their CTOs to use it, and I maintain it in production.
What I learned
AI is most useful when it suggests and clear rules decide. That is what makes it safe to put near tenants and landlords’ money.

Market intelligence · Pneuma Labs · 2026

An AI research assistant for Nairobi Securities Exchange analysts

The project
An AI tool that does the first round of stock research on the Nairobi Securities Exchange.
The goal
Cut the time analysts spend gathering prices, economic data and news before they can start thinking about a stock.
How we did it
I adapted an open-source framework into a team of AI “analysts” that each study one angle, argue the case for and against a stock, then pass a risk check that accounts for Kenyan inflation. It runs automatically every morning before the market opens, and switches to a backup AI provider if one goes down.
The challenges
The data comes from many scattered sources, AI providers sometimes go down, and nobody trusts an AI’s stock call unless its track record can be checked.
The outcome
Analysts in Kenya use it for research and decision support. It does the groundwork; the investment decision stays with them.
What I learned
You can’t trust an AI you can’t measure. Logging every prediction and scoring it later is what turns a clever demo into a tool people rely on.

British International Investment · Consulting · 2026

A climate-risk toolkit banks can actually use

The project
A digital version of British International Investment’s climate and nature risk toolkit for financial institutions.
The goal
Turn guidance that lived in PDFs and spreadsheets into tools that banks and investors can use in the middle of a real lending or investment decision.
How we did it
I broke the toolkit into modules and built step-by-step workbenches for each task: screening a client for climate risk, measuring the emissions linked to loans and investments, and stress-testing a portfolio. Each piece of work links institutions, clients and projects, and exports cleanly.
The challenges
The source guidance is long and technical, and the people using the tool are finance staff, not climate specialists. It had to stay faithful to the method while feeling simple.
The outcome
Risk and sustainability teams can work through the full framework and finish with a complete, shareable record of their assessment.
What I learned
Complex frameworks get used when they are broken into small guided steps. Most of the work is understanding the method well enough to make it simple.

Federal Ministry of Education, Nigeria · ProDG Studios · 2026

A national platform to move research into the market

The project
RICC, a national platform for Nigeria’s Research, Innovation and Commercialisation Council.
The goal
Connect universities, industry, innovators and regulators so more research turns into real products and services.
How we did it
I led the frontend and user experience. Before designing anything, I researched where collaboration was breaking down, then built the user journeys around those bottlenecks. The portal works offline: field agents fill in questionnaires where there is no internet, and their answers are sent once they are back online. I built the landing page, the analytics dashboards that show where innovations stall on the way to market, the evidence-verification panel and the consent controls, and I ran end-to-end testing before the national rollout.
The challenges
Many groups with different needs had to be served by one platform: universities, industry, innovators, regulators and funders. Much of the data had to be collected in places with weak or no internet, and the same innovation was often reported more than once.
The outcome
A live national platform for the Ministry of Education and TETFund, with questionnaires for every type of stakeholder, ready for a national rollout.
What I learned
Understand the problem before you build. Putting a broken paper process online only gives you a faster broken process.
Tools

  • Next.js

  • TypeScript

Personal finance · Lead developer · 2026

Chat with your finances, M-Pesa included

The project
An AI assistant that helps people in Kenya understand their money and their taxes.
The goal
Help people see where their money goes and file with KRA without stress, using records they already have.
How we did it
I led development, working with one other developer. People upload M-Pesa statements, bank statements and payslips, even password-protected or scanned ones. The system reads every transaction, and people can ask questions on WhatsApp, Telegram or the web. It also reads P9 tax forms and walks people through filing.
The challenges
Statements arrive in every shape: password-protected, scanned, or formatted differently by each bank. Tax rules also had to be explained in plain language.
The outcome
A working product with subscription payments.
What I learned
Meet people where they already are. M-Pesa statements and WhatsApp beat asking anyone to learn a new app.
