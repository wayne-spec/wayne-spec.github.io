National Innovation Portal (RICC)

Federal Ministry of Education · Nigeria · Lead Frontend Engineer · Jan 2026 – Jun 2026

Result Live at ricc.education.gov.ng on the government's own servers, and grown from a national survey into a full innovator-to-funding pipeline. Historical data was cleaned so 62 submissions became 298 distinct innovations, with 105 duplicates removed.

Problem and goal. Nigeria had no reliable view of how research moves from universities, institutes and innovators into the market. Data came in through Google Forms, often from areas with weak or no internet, duplicate reports were common, and funders had no way to find investable work. The goal was one national platform that collects trustworthy data from every stakeholder group, shows where innovations stall, and connects the best of them to funding.

Key contributions

  • Research: found why universities, industry, investors and regulators weren't connecting, and turned that into user journeys and stakeholder questionnaires.
  • Frontend lead: main frontend engineer across almost every module, alongside the backend lead, with 93 merged pull requests between January and June 2026.
  • Offline-first data collection: field agents complete long questionnaires with no connection, and answers sync automatically once they're back online. The questionnaires hide questions that don't apply and include repeating groups, matrices, file uploads, word limits, ranked choices, location logic, consent tracking and reference numbers.
  • Sign-off and verification: e-signing by the respondent and an authorising officer, send-back for corrections, an evidence-verification panel for the secretariat, and signed PDF receipts.
  • Analytics:
    • a Nigeria heat map and a "valley of death" chart showing where innovations drop off
    • readiness scorecards, funding maps, sector and return-on-investment charts
    • a qualitative tagging dashboard and a chatbot that answers questions about the data with live charts
    • Excel exports, after replacing a vulnerable spreadsheet library
  • Innovators and IP: registration and profiles, a claim flow for existing records, IP readiness with document upload, an inventorship register with co-inventor consent, trade-secret access behind an NDA, and technology-readiness self-assessments with eligibility results.
  • Matchmaking and funding:
    • a "For You" feed matching innovators to funders, and an innovation feed for funders (save, dismiss, express interest)
    • funder team management and the admin funding-agreement and milestone set-up
    • three-party milestone approval, dispute threads and a funded-innovator dashboard
    • the M&E dashboard and public record pages
  • Testing and security: led about 30 bug-fix pull requests in the June QA cycle, including security fixes (path traversal, spreadsheet formula injection, personal data in audit logs), and wrote the pre-handover QA pack of 142 test cases across 20 modules.

NGAO: WhatsApp Data Collection Platform

Last-mile reporting · Kenya · Full-Stack Developer · Mar 2026 – Jun 2026 (development); rolling out

Result Development finished in June 2026; rolled out and growing, with more than 3,000 users.

Problem and goal. Field reporting such as tree planting and seedling sales ran on paper and informal messages. It was slow, hard to verify and easy to fake. The goal was to let people report through WhatsApp, with every submission kept as a permanent record administrators can trust.

Key contributions

  • Conversation engine: built the engines and orchestrators that run each conversation. Flows are button-led, in English and Kiswahili, and accept text, photos, voice notes and location.
  • Answer correction: misspelled answers are matched against a dictionary first, then AI. The person confirms ("Did you mean…?"), and the engine learns from every confirmation.
  • Photo checks: each photo's location data is compared with the shared location, and mismatches are flagged without blocking the reporter.
  • Dashboards: connected the backend to the admin dashboards, and wrote the reporting reference that checks every dashboard figure against the raw records and the CSV export.
  • No-code pipelines: a schema builder lets administrators launch new pipelines (tree planting, seedling marketplace, purchasing) without new code.

Kenya Climate & Nature Directory: Research and Publishing Pipeline

Climate Shapers Africa · Africa · Sole Engineer · Jul 2026 – present

Result Running in production every night inside free tiers; the only running cost is the domain.

Problem and goal. Keeping the directory current was manual: searching Google for PDFs, judging relevance by eye, typing metadata by hand, reconciling authors in Excel, uploading covers one by one and loading the site ten rows at a time. The goal was to automate everything except the chief editor's final approval, at almost no running cost.

Key contributions

  • Discovery: a nightly pipeline searches six research sources (World Bank, OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, CORE), removes duplicates, downloads and checks each PDF, extracts the text and makes a cover image.
  • AI review: AI decides whether each document is about Kenya, East Africa or Africa and climate, keeping a reason for every rejection, then extracts the title, year, publisher, authors, document type, abstract and themes, with OCR for scanned pages and automatic switch-over between AI providers.
  • Human approval: rules in code check every result before it joins the chief editor's queue. The editor approves, edits or rejects with a reason, and approval publishes the record and rebuilds the public site.
  • Reliability: each document saves its progress, so a crash or a used-up AI quota simply resumes on the next run. Weekly link checks, author enrichment, record repair and database backups run on their own.
  • Migration: moved 2,774 existing records into a new database with row-level security.
  • Beyond the directory:
    • News Studio: daily climate stories written from the source text, with AI-checked header images
    • Content Studio: turns papers into LinkedIn posts, carousels and explainer videos
    • an opportunity tracker that emails Climate Shapers a daily digest of relevant grants and tenders
    • everything an AI writes waits for human approval

Breach Alert: Dark-Web and Data-Breach Monitoring

Individuals and companies · Global · Sole Engineer · Sep 2026

Result Went live covering 169 ransomware leak sites; its first test account surfaced one critical and two high-severity findings.

Problem and goal. People and companies usually find out their passwords or data are in criminal hands too late, and too vaguely, to act on the same day. The goal was alerts precise enough to act on immediately.

Key contributions

  • Proof of ownership: people verify what they monitor: their sign-in email, extra addresses by a time-limited six-digit code, and company domains by a DNS record.
  • Three data sources: breach databases, infostealer malware logs and ransomware leak-site listings. Duplicates are removed, each finding gets a severity, and every alert comes with plain-language next steps for people and for companies.
  • Dark-web watcher: a separate service that reads only approved leak-site pages over Tor, never follows links or downloads files, keeps only masked extracts, and matches against monitored addresses "blind" using one-way fingerprints, so it never holds the watchlist.
  • Privacy by design: emails are encrypted at rest and matched by keyed fingerprints, so a copy of the database reveals nothing; passwords are checked by sending only five characters of their hash and are never stored.
  • Operations: urgent alerts go out immediately and minor ones in a weekly summary; an admin review desk holds uncertain matches until a person approves them; 114 automated tests; three services deployed with health checks.

Harambee Shop: Group Buying at Wholesale Prices

Households and students · Kenya · Sole Engineer and Product Owner · Sep 2026 – present

Result Deployed and running a controlled pilot around Strathmore and Madaraka, with real M-Pesa payments.

Problem and goal. In Kenya, wholesale prices run 13–33% below retail, but only in bulk that a single household can't afford or store. The goal was to let neighbours buy wholesale together, safely, with M-Pesa.

Key contributions

  • Shared baskets: a group fills whole supplier packs together, each person takes single items and pays their share by M-Pesa, and the goods are bought wholesale and delivered on Saturday.
  • Pricing engine: passes half of every saving to the customer, with a discount ladder that rewards taking more of a pack, and an audit log on every money and pricing decision.
  • Payments: verified M-Pesa confirmations, account credit, re-pricing when a basket closes, refunds, and cash-outs to M-Pesa with automatic retries.
  • Pilot operations: imported and checked a real supplier catalogue against retail prices, set owner-approved risk limits, and built the Saturday purchase planner, receiving checks and delivery tracking.
  • Testing: test programs for the backend, about 40 API test suites, failure-forcing mocks for payments and messaging, and browser tests with zero accessibility findings on mobile and desktop.
